      L1: Riemann integration and its limitations 2026-08-17

      Review: the Riemann integral; theorem+proof: continuous functions are Riemann integrable; review: continuous functions, uniformly continuous functions, modulus of continuity; theorem (statement only): a function is Riemann integrable if and only if the set of its discontinuities has Lebesgue measure zero; anticipation of definition - Lebesgue measure zero: countable covers by intervals with small total lenght; the norm L1 on continuous functions; review: definition of norm; remark: L1 is not a norm on Riemann Integrable functions because separation axiom fails; Proposition (no proof yet): continuous functions with L1 norm are not a complete metric space.

      LN: Review of Riemann integration, Sections 1–2. Optional: Axler, Chapter 1;

      L2: Countability and unbounded functions 2026-08-19

      Proposition + proof: continuous functions with L1 norm are not a complete metric space. Review: existence and uniqueness of completion and extension of uniformly continuous maps; the Lebesgue integral as the completion of and the Riemann integrals with respect to . The fat rationals.

      LN: Review of Riemann integration, Sections 3–4. Optional: Axler, Chapter 1;

      L3: Countability, the extended reals, and sums of non-negative numbers 2026-08-21

      Review: fat rationals; sketch: Riemann integrable functions with respect to are incomplete. Finite, countable, and uncountable sets; countable unions and products. Cardinality Extended nonnegative reals. Arbitrary sums of non-negative quantities: definition. Countability of support of sums that are finite. Continuity of arbitrary sums wrt to increasing subsets.

      LN: Cardinality, countability, and non-negative sums.

      L4: Outer measures Tentative 2026-08-24

      Review: continuity of arbitrary non-negative sums with respect to increasing index sets Corollary: every non-negative sum coincides with its series (according to any ordering). Theorem+proof: Tonelli for non-negative sums. Overview: homework exercises on monotone convergence and dominated convergence for sums. Outer measures: motivation, defintion, examples. Generating an outer measure using a cost function. Prop+proof: generated outer measures are outer measures (-subadditivity). Maximality of generated outer measure.

      LN: Cardinality, countability, and non-negative sums, Sections 4–6.

      L5: Examples and Lebesgue outer measure on Tentative 2026-08-26

      Defn: Lebsegue outer measure on Compute outer measures generated by singleton costs and by counting cost. Prop: an outer measure extends the cost if the cost is subadditive Prop: rectangle cost is subadditive.

      L6: algebras and measures Tentative 2026-08-28

      Defn: additivity The Vitali counterexample. Motivations for measures. Defn: algebra, measure. Finite additivity, additivity, superadditivity, subadditivity.

      L7: The volume of a rectangle Tentative 2026-08-31

      Prove that the Lebesgue outer measure of a rectangle is its volume, using compactness and finite rectangular subdivisions for the lower bound. Discuss the one- and two-dimensional pictures explicitly. Deduce translation invariance and compare definitions using open, closed, and half-open rectangles.

      Axler, pp. 20–24.

      L8: The target: countable additivity Tentative 2026-09-02

      Contrast finite additivity, countable additivity, countable subadditivity, and countable superadditivity. Prove that a non-negative finitely additive set functional is countably superadditive on a disjoint family whenever all relevant unions lie in its domain. Hence, for an outer measure, finite additivity on a chosen domain supplies the missing inequality and implies countable additivity there. Test the criterion on counting measure and on separated rectangles; explain why arbitrary subsets remain problematic.

      Ambrosio–Da Prato–Mennucci, Section 1.3.

      L9: The Vitali obstruction Tentative 2026-09-04

      Construct a Vitali set in using equivalence modulo rational translation. Use translation invariance, disjoint rational translates, and countable subadditivity to prove that Lebesgue outer measure cannot be countably additive on every subset of . Identify the role of choice and formulate the repair: restrict the domain to a sufficiently rich collection of measurable sets.

      Axler, Propositions 2.18 and 2.22.

      L10: Algebras, -algebras, and generators Tentative 2026-09-09

      Define rings, algebras, and -algebras and prove their standard closure properties. Work examples: the trivial and discrete -algebras, finite partitions, finite/cofinite sets, and countable/cocountable sets. Construct the smallest -algebra containing a family by intersection, and contrast explicit generation of an algebra with the usually implicit generation of a -algebra.

      Ambrosio–Da Prato–Mennucci, Sections 1.1–1.2.

      L11: Measures and their basic inequalities Tentative 2026-09-11

      Define measures on -algebras. Derive finite additivity, monotonicity, subtraction when the smaller set has finite measure, and countable subadditivity. Disjointify a general countable union. Prove that, for a finitely additive non-negative set function, countable additivity is equivalent to continuity from below.

      Ambrosio–Da Prato–Mennucci, Section 1.3.

      5

      L12: Continuity of measures and Borel sets Tentative 2026-09-14

      Prove continuity from below for arbitrary measures and continuity from above under the necessary finite-measure hypothesis; give a counterexample when that hypothesis is removed. Define set liminf and limsup. Define the Borel -algebra of a topological space and show that on it is generated equivalently by open sets, closed sets, open rectangles, or a countable basis of rational rectangles.

      Ambrosio–Da Prato–Mennucci, Sections 1.2–1.3.

      L13: Carathéodory measurability I Tentative 2026-09-16

      Motivate the splitting criterion from the missing superadditive inequality. Define Carathéodory measurable sets for an outer measure. Prove that the measurable sets contain the empty set, are closed under complements and finite unions, and that the outer measure is finitely additive on disjoint measurable sets. For Lebesgue outer measure, begin the geometric proof that rectangles satisfy the criterion.

      Ambrosio–Da Prato–Mennucci, Section 1.5.2.

      L14: Carathéodory measurability II Tentative 2026-09-18

      Follow a fixed proof roadmap: first obtain the finite disjoint-union identity, then iterate it, and finally pass to the limit using countable subadditivity. Conclude that the Carathéodory measurable sets form a -algebra and that the restricted outer measure is a complete measure. Complete the geometric proof that rectangles are measurable; deduce that every Borel set and every subset of a null set is Lebesgue measurable. Distinguish Borel, Lebesgue measurable, and arbitrary subsets of .

      Ambrosio–Da Prato–Mennucci, Section 1.5.2.

      L15: -finite measures and Dynkin’s – theorem Tentative 2026-09-21

      Define finite and -finite measures, -systems, and Dynkin (-)systems. Prove Dynkin’s – theorem in modular steps: fix one set and form the class of compatible sets, then repeat with the roles reversed. Compare this with generation by a -algebra and explain why closure under intersections is the key hypothesis.

      Instructor notes on the – theorem.

      L16: Uniqueness of measures Tentative 2026-09-23

      Prove the uniqueness criterion for two finite measures agreeing on a generating -system. Extend it to -finite measures using an exhausting sequence of finite-measure sets from the -system. Apply the theorem to Borel measures on determined by rectangles. Synthesize the construction chain: costs, outer measure, Carathéodory domain, measure, and uniqueness.

      Instructor notes on uniqueness of measures.

      L17: Measurable functions Tentative 2026-09-25

      Begin the next unit. Define measurable spaces and measurable functions, including Borel and Lebesgue measurable real-valued functions. Use pullbacks and generators to reduce measurability checks; prove that continuous functions are Borel measurable.

      Ambrosio–Da Prato–Mennucci, Sections 2.1–2.2.

      7

      L18: Operations and limits of measurable functions Tentative 2026-09-28

      Characterize real-valued measurability using level sets. Prove closure under algebraic operations, maxima, minima, positive and negative parts, and pointwise liminf, limsup, and limits. Treat extended-real-valued functions and the conventions needed at infinity.

      Ambrosio–Da Prato–Mennucci, Sections 2.1–2.3.

      L19: Simple-function approximation Tentative 2026-09-30

      Define indicator and simple measurable functions. Construct an increasing sequence of non-negative simple functions converging pointwise to any non-negative measurable function, with explicit dyadic range truncation. Practice representing a simple function using disjoint level sets and prepare the definition of its integral.

      Ambrosio–Da Prato–Mennucci, Section 2.3, especially Proposition 2.4.

      L20: Distribution functions and layer cake Tentative 2026-10-02

      Introduce superlevel-set distribution functions and prove their monotonicity, right continuity, and existence of left limits. Develop the Archimedean integral and the layer-cake viewpoint through explicit examples, preparing the non-negative Lebesgue integral.

      Ambrosio–Da Prato–Mennucci, Sections 2.4.2–2.4.3.
