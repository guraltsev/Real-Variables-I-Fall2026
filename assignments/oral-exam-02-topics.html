    Oral Exam 2 Information#

    Last updated: October 24, 2026.

    Scheduling and exam format#

    Scheduling details and the appointment deadline will be announced.

    When you arrive for the exam, you will be given a choice of two topics from the list below. You will select one topic and have up to 30 minutes to prepare your exposition. This preparation period is closed book: you may not use notes, books, or other resources.

    After the preparation period, you will have an approximately 30-minute discussion at the board. You may consult any notes you produced during your preparation time.

    During the board discussion, you should be prepared to:

    • give a coherent overview of the selected topic and explain how its main ideas fit together;

    • state definitions and mathematical statements precisely;

    • present the central proofs at the level of their overall structure;

    • supply detailed arguments for individual steps when asked; and

    • respond to questions about hypotheses, examples, counterexamples, and connections among the results.

    A presentation does not have to be flawless to earn full marks. The goal is to demonstrate that you have understood the topic and can think about it at the board. Tips or suggestions may be provided if you get stuck. If you are receptive to suggestions and recover from a temporary hesitation, you can still earn full marks.

    Example grading rubric#

    The exam will be assessed holistically. The following descriptions illustrate the level of performance associated with each score range.

    • 90–100%: Demonstrates a strong and connected understanding of the topic. Definitions and statements are precise when requested, the main proof ideas are correct, and detailed arguments are supplied with only minor omissions or errors. Responds thoughtfully to questions and can use a tip to recover from hesitation. The presentation need not be perfect.

    • 80–89%: Demonstrates good understanding of the main ideas and proof structure. Most definitions and statements are accurate, but there are some gaps, imprecisions, or errors of substance. Responds productively to questions and suggestions, though some issues remain unresolved.

    • 70–79%: Demonstrates substantial but incomplete understanding. Can explain several central ideas and parts of the proofs, but important definitions, hypotheses, connections, or proof steps are missing or incorrect. Follow-up questions reveal uneven command of the topic. Can carry out at least one technical proof to completion.

    • 60–69%: Demonstrates limited understanding. Can state some relevant definitions or results and make partial progress on proofs, but the overall structure is unclear or contains errors. Substantial prompting is needed to proceed.

    • 50–59%: Demonstrates limited understanding. Some relevant facts are recalled, but central definitions, statements, and proof ideas are seriously incomplete or inaccurate. Makes little progress even with significant guidance.

    • Below 50%: Does not yet demonstrate a workable understanding of the selected topic.
