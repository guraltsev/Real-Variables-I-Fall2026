      8. Properties of the Lebesgue Integral#

      The layer-cake construction in the preceding notes defines the integral of every non-negative measurable function and proves monotone convergence and positive linearity. We now organize the consequences that make the integral useful in calculations: approximation from below, integration over measurable sets, countable sums, estimates for large-value sets, and invariance under changes on null sets.

      After studying these notes, you will be able to do the following.

      • Compute a non-negative integral as a supremum of simple-function integrals.

      • Integrate over a measurable subset and pass to increasing unions of domains.

      • Exchange a non-negative series with an integral.

      • Apply Markov’s inequality to estimate superlevel sets.

      • Determine when almost-everywhere changes preserve an integral.

      Summary#

      For non-negative measurable functions, the integral is monotone, respects increasing limits, and exchanges with non-negative series. It can be computed as a supremum over simple functions below the integrand. Markov’s inequality turns an integral bound into a superlevel-set estimate, and it implies that null-set changes do not alter an integral.
