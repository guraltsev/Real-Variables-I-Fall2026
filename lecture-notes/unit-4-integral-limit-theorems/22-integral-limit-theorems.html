      9. Integral limit theorems#

      Pointwise convergence alone does not determine what happens to integrals: a sequence can move its mass farther out in the space or concentrate it on smaller sets. The dominated convergence theorem rules out both behaviors by placing every term under one integrable function. We prove the theorem in two ways. The first proof uses Fatou’s lemma, while the second combines Egorov’s theorem with uniform control of an integrable function on sets of small measure.

      After studying these notes, you will be able to do the following.

      • Recognize when the dominated convergence theorem applies and compare its hypotheses with monotone convergence.

      • Construct sequences showing why pointwise convergence does not by itself imply convergence of integrals.

      • Deduce dominated convergence from Fatou’s lemma.

      • Prove Egorov’s theorem and explain why its finite-measure hypothesis is needed.

      • Prove dominated convergence by combining a finite-measure reduction, Egorov’s theorem, and uniform integrability of the dominating function.
